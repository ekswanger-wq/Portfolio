Context

Every company has a course like this one

Mandatory, annual, built by committee, and dreaded by everyone who has to click through it. Warehouse Safety Fundamentals is a fictional stand-in for a pattern I keep seeing in real compliance training: a good learning objective buried under a decade of bolted-on features.

Each department wanted something added — the LMS vendor wanted gamification, legal wanted a wall of disclaimer text, and IT wanted every navigation option visible at all times, just in case. Nobody owned the experience as a whole, and it shows the moment you open a module.

Diagnosis

Five ways the design works against the content

I ran this through a standard heuristic pass — Nielsen's usability principles, a WCAG contrast check, and a simple word count per screen — before touching any pixels.

No visual hierarchy

The warning banner, the title, and the quiz all compete at the same volume. Nothing tells the eye where to land first, so learners scan randomly instead of reading in order.

Cognitive overload

Legal disclaimer, instructional copy, a quiz, and navigation all appear on one screen. Each would be fine alone; together they ask the learner to context-switch every few seconds.

Disorienting navigation

A code like "3.2.1.b" tells a learner nothing about where they are or how much is left. The sidebar shows the whole tree at once, which advertises how long the course is rather than helping anyone move through it.

Accessibility gaps

Autoplaying video with no captions excludes anyone in a shared space or with a hearing loss. Light grey text on white and a tiny video player fail basic contrast and target-size guidelines.

Motivation aimed at the wrong thing

Points and levels reward clicking through, not understanding fire safety. When the badge is unrelated to the skill, it teaches people to chase the badge instead.

The redesign

One idea, one action, per screen

The "After" view in the toggle above is the same content, restructured. Nothing was cut for the sake of looking simple — the legal and safety content still exists, just moved to a reference page a learner can return to instead of scrolling past it mid-lesson.

The course name and step count replace the six-item nav bar. A single teal accent marks anything interactive, so there's never a question of which button actually moves you forward. Captions are on by default, and the video only plays when someone chooses to play it.

What changed, and why

Dense legal paragraph in small grey text Low contrast plus wall-of-text invites skimming, not reading Short paragraphs at readable size, legal detail moved to a reference page

Reflection

What I'd want to test next

A cleaner screen is a hypothesis, not proof. If this were a real course, I'd want to check:

  • Whether time-to-completion actually drops, or whether learners just click through faster without absorbing more
  • Whether comprehension on the post-course quiz improves, not just completion rates
  • Whether removing badges changes engagement for the group that liked them, even if the safety content improves for everyone else
This is a personal, unpaid redesign exercise built around a fictional course and company. It's meant to show how I diagnose and restructure a messy interface, not to represent a real client engagement or real outcome data.
*Meridian LMS and Warehouse Safety Fundamentals are fictional, created for this exercise.
